    Independent review corrections — November 21, 2026¶

    This repair pass addresses the November 19 and November 21 independent reviews. The original execution logs and source snapshots are preserved. A historical PASS describes its archived source, not a later corrected program.

    GPU revalidation completed¶

    The changed complete sources were compiled and executed on November 21 on a two-H100 host. All 26 programs that fit this host executed successfully; the separate attention reference passed seven complete O/LSE checks. Four corrected synchronization examples passed memcheck, racecheck and synccheck; the attention reference also passed memcheck. See dated results and raw evidence. The eight-GPU hybrid-training workload remains unexecuted on this host. These runs do not execute every explanatory protocol sketch or prove every code path.

    The Chapter 2 fixes protect remote shared-memory readers before CTA exit and remove the special-register output race. Chapter 5 adds an async-proxy fence and alignment to the reduction source. Chapter 11 replaces the self-initializing error macro. Other source edits correct explanatory comments or output labels; their current identities are included in the new GPU rerun.

    Historical evidence and scope¶

    Four archived copies of the broken Chapter 11 macro remain intentionally unchanged, documenting exactly what ran. Use the corrected current chapter source for future runs. The old compiler warnings and zero exits do not validate that error-checking path. The B200 compatibility page separates seven CPU-only checks from 29 GPU-using programs and identifies five architecture exclusions. Its native CUTLASS appendix has a distinct test set.

    Evidence verifiers check hashes, commands, dates, inventories and captured results. They do not independently attest hardware identity or rerun kernels.

    Review ID Correction

    B2-Ch02-2 Multicast sketch distinguishes cluster initialization, per-destination transaction barriers, mask and completion waits.
    B2-Ch03-1 Named barriers count all 192 participating producer/consumer threads and separate ready/release generations.
    B2-Ch04-1 128-byte-swizzled BF16 example uses a 64-element contiguous extent; descriptor, byte budget and walkthrough agree.

    B3-Ch10-1 CUTLASS fragments are traversed backwards, distinct from the paper's tile-start-owner protocol. The small role demo does not test that scheduler.
    B4-Ch11-1 Optional NVLS is distinguished from ring/tree and does not make eight-endpoint AllReduce a guaranteed one-peer transfer.
    B4-Ch11-2 Per-endpoint traffic is separated from aggregate fabric traffic; the false O(N)-to-O(1) claim is removed.
    B4-Ch11-3 Ring bandwidth and tree latency characteristics are corrected; selection is per collective.
    B4-Ch11-17 Top-K activates K experts; equal-sized top-2 experts cost approximately twice one dense FFN, independent of expert count.
    B4-Ch11-18 Tuning starts with NCCL's defaults and observed selection; invented SHARP settings and reversed protocol advice are removed.
    B4-Ch12-1 Pending PV completes before O rescaling or V-slot release; the final PV contribution is drained. This is a protocol sketch, not a newly executed FA3 implementation.

    Deliberate limits and retained records¶

    The redirect stubs are retained to preserve published external URLs; deleting these is optional housekeeping, not a correctness fix. Archived source copies, commands, logs, hashes and frozen execution summaries are not rewritten. The build adds a historical-record notice to those summary pages. The attention acceptance tolerance remains an explicit acceptance contract, not a claim about the observed maximum error. Source/log consistency cannot prove hardware attestation. No missing capture driver, tarball, optimized benchmark, two-SM UMMA run, PyTorch extension or GPU result has been invented.

    Framework integration is documented as a contract, not a compiled extension. The Python workbooks remain limited teaching models and are no longer cited as concurrent-protocol tests. Those are scope corrections rather than new execution results. The FlashAttention implementation-reading reference now names an exact upstream commit.
